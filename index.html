The thesis.

Master's in Cyber Security · Detecting Mirai botnet attacks on IoT with a hybrid autoencoder + GRU model, tested on a real lab I built and attacked.

The test bed: Raspberry Pi 4 and its sensors

The lab

A smart-farm node that reports readings and photos every few seconds, the kind of device Mirai goes after.

  • Raspberry Pi 4 with camera, PIR motion, HC-SR04 distance, flame, temperature & humidity sensors
  • Readings published over MQTT (port 1883) to a Node.js server
  • Attack side: Kali Linux VMs running the Mirai source, a C2 server and two bots, isolated on a local network

The attack map

A replay of the lab, step by step. Click a step or let it run. All of this happened on isolated virtual machines, never on a real network.

The model

An autoencoder built from GRU layers learns what normal traffic looks like; a second GRU classifies the compressed features. The GRU's reset and update gates keep the time order of packets, which is what makes a flood stand out.
